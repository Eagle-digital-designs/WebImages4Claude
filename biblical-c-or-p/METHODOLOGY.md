# Methodology

## Translations

Direct scripture quotations in this library are drawn from public-domain English translations — primarily the **King James Version (KJV)** and, where noted, the **World English Bible (WEB)**. Both are free of copyright restriction, which keeps this a genuinely open reference library. Where a dispute hinges on a translation choice (a famous example: whether a Greek or Hebrew word is better rendered one way or another), that is called out explicitly rather than papered over — translation choice is sometimes part of the actual argument, not just a stylistic accident.

Readers are encouraged to cross-check every reference against a study Bible or interlinear in the original Hebrew/Greek/Aramaic before drawing conclusions, especially on disputed terms (e.g. *paradosis* "tradition" in 2 Thessalonians 2:15, *petra*/*petros* in Matthew 16:18, *episkopos* "overseer/bishop" throughout the pastoral epistles).

## How references were selected

For each topic, this library lists:

1. The passages a tradition's own catechetical or confessional documents actually cite for that doctrine (the Catechism of the Catholic Church, papal encyclicals, and Church councils on the Catholic side; the Westminster Confession, the Augsburg Confession, the Thirty-Nine Articles, and comparable Reformation-era confessions on the Protestant side) — not passages picked after the fact to sound persuasive.
2. Passages the other side raises as the strongest counter-reading of the same texts.
3. Passages both traditions read the same way, to keep the actual size of the disagreement in view — these disputes are usually narrower than they're presented.

## Neutrality principles

- **No verse is quoted out of its chapter without a pointer back to the chapter.** A one-line proof text is often the beginning of a case, not the end of one.
- **Each side is represented by its strongest form**, drawing on primary confessional/catechetical sources rather than caricatures or the weakest popular argument.
- **"Common ground" is stated for every topic.** These divisions are real, but they are narrower than internet arguments make them look, and both traditions affirm far more than they deny of each other's reading of scripture.
- **This library does not adjudicate.** It is a search tool, not a verdict. The reader does the reading.

## How to use a topic file

1. Read "The question" to know precisely what is and isn't being disputed.
2. Read both positions' primary passages in full context (open the actual chapter, not just the verse).
3. Read "Common ground" so the scope of the disagreement stays honest.
4. Follow "Search terms" for passages that complicate or sharpen the picture — these are often the ones that get left out of one-sided treatments.
5. Read at least one primary confessional document per side (linked or named in each file) rather than stopping at secondary commentary.
