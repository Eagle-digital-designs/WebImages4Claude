# Scripture and Tradition

## The question

Is Scripture the sole infallible rule of faith (*sola scriptura*), or do Scripture and apostolic Tradition, safeguarded by the Church's teaching authority (the Magisterium), together constitute the rule of faith?

## Catholic position

- **2 Thessalonians 2:15** — Paul tells believers to "stand fast, and hold the traditions which ye have been taught, whether by word, or our epistle." Both oral and written apostolic teaching are called binding "tradition" (Greek *paradosis*).
- **2 Thessalonians 3:6** — Paul again invokes "the tradition which he received of us" as authoritative.
- **1 Corinthians 11:2** — Paul commends the church "because ye... keep the ordinances [traditions], as I delivered them to you."
- **John 21:25** — the Gospel of John closes by noting Jesus did many things not written down, implying not all apostolic teaching was committed to text.
- **1 Timothy 3:15** — the Church (not Scripture alone) is called "the pillar and ground of the truth."
- **Matthew 16:18–19** and **Matthew 18:18** — binding/loosing authority given to Peter and the apostles is read as establishing a living teaching office that continues to authoritatively interpret revelation.
- **Acts 15** (the Council of Jerusalem) — a doctrinal dispute is settled not by private scripture reading alone but by an authoritative council of apostles and elders, whose decision is then delivered to the churches as binding ("it seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us," Acts 15:28).
- Catholic theology also notes that the New Testament canon itself was not settled by explicit scriptural self-attestation — the Church's teaching authority discerned and affirmed it in the 4th century, which is read as evidence that *some* authoritative interpretive body was always assumed to be operating alongside the text.

Primary source: *Catechism of the Catholic Church* §74–95 ("The Transmission of Divine Revelation").

## Protestant position

- **2 Timothy 3:16–17** — "All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: that the man of God may be perfect, thoroughly furnished unto all good works." Read as scripture being sufficient to fully equip a believer, leaving no gap that a separate, unwritten tradition must fill.
- **Acts 17:11** — the Bereans are commended as "more noble" than the Thessalonians because they tested even the apostle Paul's own preaching "searching the scriptures daily, whether those things were so." Scripture is treated as the standard even apostolic teaching is checked against.
- **Mark 7:8–9, 13** — Jesus rebukes the Pharisees for laying aside the commandment of God to hold "the tradition of men," warning that tradition can nullify the word of God rather than transmit it faithfully.
- **Galatians 1:8** — Paul pronounces a curse on anyone, "though we or an angel from heaven," who preaches a gospel contrary to what was already delivered, read as closing off any later addition that could alter apostolic doctrine.
- **Revelation 22:18–19** — a solemn warning against adding to or taking away from "the words of the prophecy of this book," cited (though addressed specifically to Revelation) as expressing a broader principle against supplementing revelation.
- Protestant theology reads 2 Thessalonians 2:15 and 1 Corinthians 11:2 as referring to apostolic-era oral teaching that was itself later written down and preserved *in* scripture, not to an ongoing, separate, extra-biblical stream of revelation continuing to develop after the apostolic age closed.

Primary sources: *Westminster Confession of Faith* I.1–I.10; *Thirty-Nine Articles* Article VI ("Of the Sufficiency of the Holy Scriptures for Salvation").

## Common ground

Both traditions affirm Scripture is inspired, inerrant in what it teaches, and authoritative. Both affirm the apostles taught orally before (and alongside) writing scripture. Neither side treats "tradition" as meaning merely human custom — the dispute is specifically about whether normative, binding apostolic teaching continued to be transmitted through a living Church authority after the apostolic age, or whether it was fully and finally deposited in the written text.

## Search terms

- Matthew 15:1–9 (tradition vs. commandment, parallel to Mark 7)
- Colossians 2:8 ("philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men")
- 1 Corinthians 15:1–3 (the gospel itself described as something "received" and "delivered," i.e. tradition)
- Jude 1:3 ("the faith which was once delivered unto the saints")
- Deuteronomy 4:2 and 12:32 (Old Testament precedent against adding to or subtracting from the commandments)
- Nehemiah 8 (Ezra's public reading and communal interpretation of the Law)
