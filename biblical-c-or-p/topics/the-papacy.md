# The Papacy

## The question

Did Christ establish a perpetual, authoritative office held first by Peter and then by his successors as universal shepherd of the Church — or was Peter's role unique to the apostolic founding generation, with no biblically mandated succession?

## Catholic position

- **Matthew 16:17–19** — Jesus renames Simon "Peter" (Greek *Petros*, "rock"), saying "upon this rock (*petra*) I will build my church," gives him "the keys of the kingdom of heaven," and grants binding/loosing authority. Read as a personal, unique commission establishing Peter as the foundation-officer of the visible Church, with the "keys" evoking the royal steward office of Isaiah 22:20–22.
- **Luke 22:31–32** — Jesus tells Peter specifically, "I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren," read as a distinct pastoral commission over the other apostles.
- **John 21:15–17** — the risen Christ commissions Peter three times, "Feed my lambs... feed my sheep," a shepherding charge over the whole flock, not merely his own converts.
- Peter is consistently named first in every New Testament list of apostles (e.g. Matthew 10:2), speaks first and most prominently at Pentecost (Acts 2) and at the Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15:7), and his historical presence and martyrdom in Rome is treated by Catholic tradition as the basis for the bishop of Rome inheriting his pastoral office (apostolic succession).
- Catholic theology holds the office (not personal impeccability) continues: successive bishops of Rome inherit Peter's Petrine ministry of unity and doctrinal confirmation for the universal Church.

Primary source: *Catechism of the Catholic Church* §880–882; First Vatican Council, *Pastor Aeternus* (1870).

## Protestant position

- On Matthew 16:18, the Reformation-era reading (also held by some early church fathers) distinguishes *Petros* (a stone) from *petra* (bedrock), arguing the "rock" is Peter's confession that Jesus is "the Christ, the Son of the living God" (Matthew 16:16) rather than Peter's person or office — Christ himself, or the apostolic confession of Christ, is the foundation (compare 1 Corinthians 3:11, "other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ").
- **Ephesians 2:20** — the Church is described as "built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone" — a plural, collegial foundation of *all* the apostles, not Peter singularly.
- **Galatians 2:11–14** — Paul publicly rebukes Peter "to the face" for hypocrisy over table fellowship with Gentiles, read as inconsistent with Peter holding an office of supreme, unquestionable teaching authority even within his own lifetime.
- **1 Peter 5:1–4** — Peter himself, writing to fellow elders, calls himself "also an elder" among them rather than asserting a unique supreme office, and instructs elders not to lord authority "as being lords over God's heritage" but to lead by example, awaiting "the chief Shepherd" (Christ).
- **Acts 15** — James, not Peter, appears to render the Council's summary judgment (Acts 15:13–21, "my sentence is..."), read as evidence leadership in the earliest Church was collegial/local rather than centered in a single perpetual Petrine office.
- No New Testament text explicitly institutes a mechanism of succession for Peter's role to specific future individuals; the Protestant reading holds any unique commission Peter received belonged to his own apostolic ministry and ended with the apostolic age.

Primary sources: *Westminster Confession of Faith* XXV.6 (denying the pope's claim to be head of the Church); John Calvin, *Institutes of the Christian Religion* IV.6.

## Common ground

Both traditions agree Peter held a position of visible prominence among the Twelve and played a leading role in the earliest Church described in Acts. Both affirm Christ alone is the ultimate foundation and head of the Church. The dispute is specifically about whether Peter's prominence constituted an *office* transmissible to named successors, or a *role* particular to his own life and ministry.

## Search terms

- Isaiah 22:15–25 (the "key of the house of David" background image behind Matthew 16:19)
- Matthew 18:18 (binding/loosing language given to the disciples generally, not Peter alone)
- Acts 8:14, Acts 10–11 (instances of Peter being sent by / accountable to the Jerusalem apostles)
- 2 Peter 1:1 and 1 Peter 1:1 (how Peter identifies himself in his own letters)
- John 20:21–23 (a similar Spirit-giving and forgiving/retaining-of-sins commission given to the disciples as a group)
