# Justification

## The question

Is a person justified before God by faith alone (*sola fide*), with good works following as evidence but contributing nothing to justification itself — or is a person justified by faith working through love, such that grace-empowered works are part of (not merely evidence of) the justification process?

## Catholic position

- **James 2:24** — "Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only." Read as a direct, explicit statement that faith alone does not justify.
- **James 2:14–26** — the fuller passage: faith without works is called "dead," and Abraham is said to have been justified by works when he offered Isaac (James 2:21), read alongside Genesis 22.
- **Galatians 5:6** — "faith which worketh by love" — justifying faith is described as inherently active, not inert.
- **Romans 2:6–13** — God "will render to every man according to his deeds," and it is "the doers of the law" who "shall be justified," read as integrating works into the final verdict of justification, not just sanctification after the fact.
- **Matthew 25:31–46** (the sheep and the goats) — the final judgment is described in terms of deeds done or not done.
- **Philippians 2:12** — believers are told to "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling," read as describing an ongoing, cooperative process rather than a single completed transaction.
- Catholic theology distinguishes initial justification (a pure, unearned gift of grace, received through faith, to which no prior human merit contributes) from the increase and preservation of that justified state, in which grace-enabled works genuinely participate.

Primary source: *Catechism of the Catholic Church* §1987–2029; Council of Trent, *Decree on Justification* (1547).

## Protestant position

- **Romans 3:28** — "Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law."
- **Romans 4:1–5** — Abraham is justified by believing God, "and it was counted unto him for righteousness," before the giving of circumcision or the Law, read as showing justification precedes and is independent of works.
- **Galatians 2:16** — "a man is not justified by the works of the law, but by the faith of Jesus Christ... that we might be justified by the faith of Christ, and not by the works of the law: for by the works of the law shall no flesh be justified."
- **Ephesians 2:8–9** — "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: not of works, lest any man should boast."
- **Titus 3:5** — "Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us."
- On James 2, the Protestant reading distinguishes justification *before God* (the legal declaration of righteousness, by faith alone) from justification *before men* (the vindication or demonstration of a real, living faith through works, which James is read as addressing) — James is answering "can that faith save him?" about a merely verbal, fruitless claim of faith, not describing the ground of justification before God.

Primary sources: *Westminster Confession of Faith* XI ("Of Justification"); Martin Luther's preface to Romans; the *Augsburg Confession* Article IV.

## Common ground

Both traditions affirm justification is by grace, through faith, made possible only by Christ's atoning death — not earned by human effort apart from grace. Both affirm that authentic faith produces good works and that a faith producing no works at all is not saving faith. The dispute is specifically about whether works are the *fruit and evidence* of a justification already fully accomplished by faith, or a *constitutive part* of an ongoing justification process.
